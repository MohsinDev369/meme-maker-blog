The Impact font is the reason most people can spot a meme from across the room. Tall white capitals, a black outline, one line at the top and one at the bottom. That look is so familiar that it reads as "joke incoming" before anyone has read a word.

This guide covers where Impact came from, how it ended up on cat photos, and the practical part: how to caption a meme so people can read it on a phone in a second or two, whether you use Impact or something else.

---

## Where the Impact Font Came From

Impact started life in print, decades before anyone made an image macro. According to [Microsoft's typography notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/font-list/impact), Geoffrey Lee designed it and the Sheffield type foundry Stephenson Blake first issued it in 1965. Lee was an advertising design director at the time, and he drew the face for posters and publicity, the kind of print that has to grab you from a distance.

The mid-1960s were a peak period for heavy, condensed lettering. Microsoft's page points out that Impact was a little less squeezed than a German face called Schmalfette Grotesk, so designers often paired the two. The goal was obvious from the name: thick strokes, tight spacing, and as much ink as possible in a small width.

The jump to screens came three decades later. Impact was one of Microsoft's original Core fonts for the Web, posted on 1 March 1996, and it shipped with Windows 98. That detail matters more than it seems. A font that sits on nearly every computer becomes the default choice for anyone making something quickly.

## How Impact Became the Meme Font

[Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/66896/why-do-all-memes-use-same-typeface) traces Impact's big break to the 2007 "I Can Haz Cheezburger" cat image, which kicked off a wave of lolcat sites. The article's point is simple: the other common system fonts, like Arial or Times New Roman, are fine on a white page but get lost on top of a busy photo. Impact's heavy letters don't.

From there, the style hardened into a template. White capitals, black stroke, top text sets up, bottom text pays off. The [Wikipedia entry on Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_(typeface)) notes that the white-with-black-outline treatment stuck because it stays readable over almost any background. It also notes that the font is now used ironically a lot of the time, which is its own kind of success. A typeface has to be very famous before it can be a joke by itself.

## Why White Text With a Black Outline Works

Meme backgrounds are unpredictable. A single image can have a bright sky in one corner and a dark jacket in another. Pure white text disappears on the sky. Pure black text disappears on the jacket.

An outline fixes that by giving every letter its own contrasting edge. White fill carries the text over dark areas, and the black stroke carries it over light areas. You never have to check which part of the photo the caption lands on.

That is also why Meme0's editor starts every new text layer as white Impact with a black stroke. It's the safe default. Change it when you have a reason to.

## How to Caption a Meme So It's Readable

Most unreadable memes fail on the same handful of things. Here is what to check before you download.

### Outline thickness

Too thin and the stroke vanishes once a platform compresses your image. Too thick and the letters fill in, especially small ones like "e" and "a". A good starting point is a stroke that looks slightly heavy at full size on your screen. It will look right once the image is shrunk into a feed.

If the image is mostly one tone, you can skip the outline and just pick a fill color that contrasts. A plain white [blank template](https://www.meme0.com/memes/blank) with black text needs no stroke at all.

### Size and line length

People see memes on phones, usually small, usually scrolling. Text that looks big on a laptop can be tiny in a feed. Two rules help:

- If a line is so long that the text has to shrink to fit, cut words instead of shrinking type.
- Keep each caption to one or two lines where you can. Three lines of Impact at the top of an image starts to cover the face, and the face is usually the joke.

### Placement

Top and bottom are traditional for a reason. Those edges are often sky, floor, wall or a blurred background, so the text doesn't sit on top of the important part. On templates with a built-in surface, like the sign in the [guy holding cardboard sign meme](https://www.meme0.com/memes/guy-holding-cardboard-sign-meme) or the paper in [Kishibe holding a note](https://www.meme0.com/memes/kishibe-holding-a-note), put the text where the object is. Black text with no stroke usually reads more naturally there, since it looks written on rather than stamped over.

Avoid covering eyes and mouths. The expression is doing half the work.

### Case

All caps is part of the classic Impact look. For newer formats, especially anime screenshots and reaction images, sentence case often feels more natural and less like a 2012 image macro. Pick one style and keep it consistent within a single meme.

### Contrast check

Before downloading, zoom out until the image is roughly phone-feed size. If you have to squint, so will everyone else. Add stroke, increase size, or move the text.

## Alternatives to Impact

You don't have to use Impact. Different fonts change the tone of a joke, and some formats look odd with classic meme text slapped on them. Meme0's editor includes several options that work well:

**Anton.** The closest stand-in for Impact. It's a tall, heavy condensed sans, so it gives you the classic meme look with slightly cleaner letter shapes. It loads as a web font in the editor, so it looks the same on any device.

**Oswald.** Also condensed, but lighter and a bit more modern. Good for longer captions where full Impact weight would feel like shouting.

**Bangers.** A comic-book display face with a slanted, punchy feel. It suits anime and manga templates, sound effects, and anything meant to feel loud on purpose. Try it on [Aizen nani](https://www.meme0.com/memes/Aizen-nani) for a reaction caption.

**CC Wild Words.** Comic-style lettering that fits neatly inside speech bubbles. Use it in bubbles rather than over the image.

**Arial Black.** Wide and heavy. It's a solid choice for labels on objects or characters, where Impact can look too narrow.

**Permanent Marker or Patrick Hand.** Handwritten looks for signs, notes and anything that should feel scrawled.

A loose rule: classic top-and-bottom formats want Impact or Anton. Label memes and screenshots often look better in a clean sans. Manga panels want comic lettering.

## A Quick Readability Checklist

1. White fill, black stroke unless the background is one flat tone.
2. One or two lines per caption.
3. Text in empty areas, never across faces.
4. Same font and case across the whole meme.
5. Zoom out and check it at phone size.

## FAQ

### What font do memes use?

Most classic memes use Impact, usually in white capitals with a black outline. Newer formats often use plain sans-serif fonts, comic lettering, or handwritten styles depending on the template.

### Who designed the Impact font?

Geoffrey Lee designed Impact, and the Stephenson Blake foundry in Sheffield released it in 1965. It was originally meant for advertising and posters.

### How do I add an outline to meme text?

In most meme editors, select the text and set a stroke color and width. In Meme0, new text already has a black stroke on white Impact, and you can change the stroke color and thickness from the text toolbar.
