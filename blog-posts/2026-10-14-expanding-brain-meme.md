The Expanding Brain meme is a vertical stack of panels. Each row pairs a caption on the left with a brain on the right, and the brain gets bigger and brighter as you go down, starting as a small gray scan and ending as a glowing cosmic thing beaming light. The captions pretend to get smarter in step. Usually they get dumber.

It's also called Galaxy Brain, after that last panel. Here is where it came from and how to write a good one.

## Where does the Expanding Brain meme come from?

The meme started as a joke about a fake word. In June 2016, an Urban Dictionary user called "lightning guy" posted a definition for "whomst," a fancier way to say "who" or "whom" for when you want to sound smarter than you are. People on Tumblr and 4chan ran with it and kept stacking suffixes: whomst, whomst'd, whomst'd've.

The brain images entered the picture on January 31, 2017. Reddit user janskishimanski posted to r/dankmemes with four rows: "who," "whom," "whomst" and "whomst'd," each next to a progressively more enlightened brain. The post got about 1,200 upvotes, according to [the meme's documented history](https://meme.com/memes/galaxy-brain/md).

That pairing is what made it stick. The "whomst" joke was already about pretending to be clever, and the glowing brains made the pretension visual.

## How it spread

Through February 2017, people on r/dankmemes and r/MemeEconomy started dropping "whomst" and swapping in other subjects. Video games and politics were early favorites. A user called BagelBoy also made a video version with an echoing voice saying "whomst'd've" over Richard Strauss's *Also sprach Zarathustra*.

By March 2017 it had crossed onto Twitter, Tumblr and Instagram, and outlets like New York Magazine and Mic had written it up. Around the same time "galaxy brain" became slang on its own. You'll see "galaxy-brained take" in political arguments online, meaning an idea that's so overthought it loops back around to wrong.

## What the meme actually means

There are two ways people use it, and they're opposites.

**The ironic version** is the common one. The panels climb from a sensible idea to a ridiculous one, and the biggest brain gets the worst take. The joke is on whoever thinks overcomplicating something makes them clever.

**The sincere version** ranks real preferences, from worst to best, with the galaxy brain actually being the smart choice. This was more common early on and is rarer now, partly because readers assume irony by default.

If you're not sure which one you're making, readers will default to irony, so write the last panel to survive that reading.

## Common variants

The four-panel layout is standard, but it isn't fixed.

- **Three panels** for a shorter joke where you just need setup, step and punchline.
- **Five or more panels** for long escalations. These can work, but every extra row has to earn its place.
- **Text-only versions** that drop the brain pictures and keep the escalating list. These show up a lot on Twitter.
- **Swapped images**, where people replace the brains with other pictures that escalate. One 2018 Instagram version used stills of Drake from a Sprite commercial, his body cracking open a little more in each panel.

The brain images themselves follow a loose pattern: a plain brain, a brain with pink or purple activity, a brain lighting up, and finally a brain merging with the cosmos.

## How to write a good Expanding Brain meme

Most Expanding Brain memes fall flat because the jumps between panels are random. A good one has a logic you can follow, even if that logic is stupid.

**Start normal.** The first panel should be the obvious, boring answer everyone agrees with. That's your baseline.

**Make each step a small, confident leap.** Each panel should sound like someone who just had a clever thought and is very proud of it. If panel three makes no sense coming after panel two, the reader stops playing along.

**Save the absurd one for the end.** The last panel is the punchline. It should be technically related to the first but obviously worse, or so overengineered it becomes funny.

**Keep captions short.** One line per panel. If you need a second sentence, the idea isn't tight enough.

Here's a quick example about watching anime:

1. Watching an episode when it comes out
2. Waiting for the season to finish so you can binge it
3. Waiting for the next season to be announced so you can binge both
4. Never starting it because the manga might get a better adaptation someday

Each step is a reasonable-sounding extension of the last, and the final one is so committed to optimizing that it skips the actual show.

## FAQ

### What is the Expanding Brain meme called?

It's known as both Expanding Brain and Galaxy Brain. Galaxy Brain technically refers to the final cosmic panel, but people use either name for the whole format.

### When did the Expanding Brain meme start?

The "whomst" joke behind it started on Urban Dictionary in June 2016. The brain version was posted to Reddit's r/dankmemes on January 31, 2017, and spread widely through February and March of that year.

### Is the Expanding Brain meme sarcastic?

Usually, yes. Most versions are ironic, with the biggest brain getting the worst idea. A sincere version that ranks real preferences also exists, but it's much less common.
