The Distracted Boyfriend meme is a stock photo. A man walking down a street with his girlfriend turns his head to stare at another woman who has just passed, while the girlfriend glares at him in disbelief. Add three labels and you have one of the most reused images of the last decade.

Unlike most memes, this one has a known photographer, a known location and a known release date. Here is the full story.

## Who took the Distracted Boyfriend photo?

The photo was taken by Antonio Guillem, a professional photographer based in Barcelona. He shot it in mid-2015 in Girona, a city in Catalonia, Spain. The setting was improvised on the street, and the people in it are models Guillem's studio works with regularly. They are known publicly only by the names Mario (the boyfriend) and Laura (the girlfriend).

It was made for stock agencies. On Shutterstock it was listed with a caption describing a disloyal man walking with his girlfriend and looking amazed at another woman. On iStock the title is close to the same. It was one image in a larger set of staged infidelity scenes the studio shot that day, and the session was planned to be playful rather than serious.

In other words, it was always meant to be generic. Stock photography exists so that a blog or ad can grab a picture of "jealous couple" without hiring anyone. That generic quality is a big part of why it became such a good meme.

## When did it become a meme?

The first known meme use dates to January 2017, in a Turkish Facebook group about progressive rock. Someone labeled the boyfriend as Phil Collins. The joke was reposted on an English-language prog rock page the next day and showed up on Twitter in early February.

It bubbled along for months at a small scale. The big moment came on August 19, 2017, when a Twitter user labeled the boyfriend "the youth," the woman walking past "socialism," and the girlfriend "capitalism." According to the [Wikipedia article on the meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distracted_boyfriend), one copy of that post was retweeted more than 35,000 times. Within a week it was everywhere, and news sites started tracking down the photographer.

By the end of 2017 it had landed on year-end "best memes" lists from outlets including The Next Web, PC Magazine and The Ringer. In April 2018 it won Best Meme at the 10th Shorty Awards.

## What the photographer said about it

Guillem found out about the meme from his own models, who saw it spreading online. In an interview with [Money](https://money.com/the-photographer-behind-the-distracted-boyfriend-meme-isnt-making-any-money-off-its-popularity/) published on August 29, 2017, he said the meme had brought the studio essentially no income, because the people making memes weren't buying licenses. He put meme-related sales at "0.00000%" of monthly revenue.

He also gave some context on the photo's normal sales. Their best-selling images sell around 5,000 to 6,000 times a year. This one sold about 700 times a year at that point.

On copyright, his position was clear. The images are licensed stock and using them without a license isn't allowed. He said the studio wouldn't go after people using the photo in good faith, but would take action over uses that were offensive or harmful to the models or to him.

## How the meme is used

The format has three labels:

- **The boyfriend** is the person or group being tempted.
- **The woman walking past** is the new, shiny option.
- **The girlfriend** is the thing they already have or should be paying attention to.

So a typical version reads: boyfriend labeled "me," passing woman labeled "starting a new anime," girlfriend labeled "the four shows I'm halfway through." The joke is always about divided attention or loyalty, and it works for almost any subject.

Over the years it has been stretched in a lot of directions. In January 2018 a biblical version about Lot's wife went viral. In March 2018 it appeared on a protest sign at a national school walkout over gun violence. People have pointed out older images with the same setup, including Joshua Reynolds's 1761 painting *David Garrick Between Tragedy and Comedy* and a scene from Charlie Chaplin's 1922 short *Pay Day*. In 2019 The New York Times ran it in the Business section to illustrate a proposed merger between Renault and Fiat Chrysler.

## When the meme got into trouble

Not every use went well. In September 2018, Sweden's advertising ombudsman ruled that an ad by internet provider Bahnhof, built on the meme, broke rules against gender discrimination. The photo itself has critics too. Joe McCarthy of Global Citizen argued that it depicts sexual harassment.

That's worth keeping in mind if you use it for a brand. Labeling people as objects is the point of the format, and it reads differently when a company does it to sell something.

## Why it lasted

Three labels is the sweet spot. Two-panel formats like the [Drake meme](https://www.meme0.com/blog/2026-05-31-drake-meme-explained) handle preference. Distracted Boyfriend adds a third party, so it can show temptation and the fallout in one frame without any text beyond the labels.

The expressions also do a lot of work. The boyfriend's open-mouthed look and the girlfriend's outrage are readable even at tiny sizes, which matters on a phone feed.

It even became a reference point for newer memes. When the anime [Is This a Pigeon](https://www.meme0.com/blog/2026-10-12-is-this-a-pigeon-meme) format exploded in 2018, Mashable called it the new Distracted Boyfriend, because both work by sticking labels on people in a single image.
