Aizen memes are built on one idea: he was always three steps ahead, and you were the last to know. Sosuke Aizen from Bleach is the villain fans love to mock and admire at the same time. His plans work out so perfectly that the story sometimes looks rigged in his favor, and the fandom turned that into a joke that has outlived the original anime run.

This post covers where the two main Aizen meme formats come from, the "nani" reaction and the "Since when were you under the impression" panel, and how people use them now.

## Who Aizen is, without the full spoiler dump

Tite Kubo created Bleach, and Aizen first shows up in Chapter 79, "Fourteen Days for Conspiracy," published in 2003, according to [Wikipedia's Aizen article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sousuke_Aizen). He is introduced as the captain of the Fifth Division, a kind, soft-spoken man in glasses.

That image is a lie. Aizen fakes his own death, quietly kills the Central 46 council and uses his sword's power to make it look like they are still alive and still giving orders, including the order to execute Rukia Kuchiki. When Retsu Unohana works it out, he leaves for Hueco Mundo with Gin Ichimaru and Kaname Tosen.

His sword, Kyoka Suigetsu, is the key to almost every Aizen meme. Its ability is called Complete Hypnosis. Anyone who sees the release of the sword even once can be fed a false version of reality whenever he wants. So any time something seems to go wrong for Aizen, there is always the possibility that it did not go wrong at all. You just saw what he wanted you to see.

In Japanese he is voiced by Sho Hayami, and in English by Kyle Hebert.

## The "Since when were you under the impression" meme

This is the most famous Aizen format. The [Aizen comic meme template](https://www.meme0.com/memes/aizen-comic-meme) shows him half lost in black shadow, eyes narrowed, with a speech bubble that reads "Since when were you under the impression that..." and an empty bubble beside it for your punchline.

The line fans quote ties back to Kyoka Suigetsu. The scene most people associate with it is in Chapter 392, "The Breaking Glaciers," in volume 45, adapted in anime episode 293. During the fight in the fake Karakura Town, Hitsugaya appears to land a sword through Aizen, and for a second everyone thinks it is over. Then it turns out the blade went through Momo Hinamori instead. Shinji demands to know how long Kyoka Suigetsu has been active, and Aizen's answer amounts to: it never stopped. A [chapter summary on DaddyJim](https://daddyjim.com/bleach/manga-chapter/392-The-Breaking-Glaciers) lays out the sequence.

That reveal is genuinely dark in context. As a meme, it becomes a way to pull the rug out from under anyone. The setup half stays fixed, and you write the reversal:

- "Since when were you under the impression that I was going to do the reading?"
- "Since when were you under the impression that this was decaf?"
- "Since when were you under the impression that the meeting could have been an email? It was always going to be two hours."

The best versions are petty. Aizen's tone is grand and theatrical, so pairing it with something tiny, like a roommate who ate your leftovers, gets the biggest reaction.

## The Aizen nani meme

"Nani" is Japanese for "what," and online it has become shorthand for a shocked anime reaction. The [Aizen nani template](https://www.meme0.com/memes/Aizen-nani) is a two-part manga panel. At the top, Aizen in his old glasses-wearing look gives a slow, knowing smile. Below him, a second character stares up wide-eyed while a blade cuts across the frame.

The image tells the whole story without words. One person is calm and in control. The other has just realized how badly they misread the situation. That makes it a natural reaction image for:

- Plot twists in shows, games or sports. "The team that was down 3-0 at halftime."
- Getting outplayed in a game by someone who seemed clueless.
- Group projects, where the quiet member turns out to have done everything.

People usually label the two figures. Aizen gets the name of whoever planned ahead, and the shocked face gets the name of whoever got caught out. Add a small "nani" near the bottom character if you want to lean into the classic anime reaction.

## Why fans keep making Aizen memes

Know Your Meme's entry on Aizen describes him as both a target of mockery and a source of admiration, mostly because of his near-perfect foresight and how often the story bends to his plans. That is the core of the joke. Fans exaggerate it to the point that Aizen gets credit for everything. Bad weather, a missed bus, the last slice of pizza being gone. It was his plan all along.

Bleach also never really left the conversation. The Thousand-Year Blood War anime, which continues the story after Aizen's arc, premiered on October 11, 2022, and its fourth and final part, The Calamity, started on July 25, 2026 and wrapped in October 2026, per [Wikipedia's TYBW page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleach:_Thousand-Year_Blood_War). Anyone starting from episode one now gets Aizen's whole long con before the final war, which is the best way to see why the memes exist.

## FAQ

### What episode is "Since when were you under the impression" from?

The scene fans tie it to is Bleach Chapter 392, "The Breaking Glaciers," which was adapted in anime episode 293. It is the moment Aizen reveals Kyoka Suigetsu was active the whole time, and Hitsugaya's attack hit Hinamori instead of him.

### What does Aizen's Kyoka Suigetsu do?

Its ability, Complete Hypnosis, lets Aizen control what someone perceives once they have seen the sword's release. He can show them a false version of events, which is why so many Aizen memes are about secret plans and fake-outs.

### What does "nani" mean in anime memes?

"Nani" means "what" in Japanese. In memes it marks a shocked reaction, usually the moment someone realizes they have been outplayed. The Aizen nani template pairs it with Aizen's calm smile to show who was really in control.
