Getting the meme image size right is boring until your caption comes out blurry or cropped in half. Every platform resizes and compresses uploads differently, and most "ultimate size guides" copy numbers from each other without saying where they came from.

This guide sticks to what each platform's own help center says. We checked every page listed below on **28 October 2026**. Where a platform doesn't publish a number, we say so instead of filling the gap with a guess. Platforms change these limits without much notice, so if something looks off, the linked help page is the place to check.

## Instagram Meme Size

Instagram is the one platform here that spells out pixel numbers. Its [help page on image resolution](https://help.instagram.com/1631821640426723) says photos are uploaded at up to 1,080 pixels wide. If your image is between 320 and 1,080 pixels wide and its aspect ratio sits between 1.91:1 and 3:4, Instagram keeps it at its original resolution. At 1,080 wide, that means a height anywhere from 566 to 1,440 pixels.

Outside that range, things happen to your image. Wider than 1,080 and it gets scaled down. Narrower than 320 and it gets enlarged, which looks soft. An aspect ratio outside the supported range gets cropped, and with a meme the crop usually eats the top or bottom caption first.

What that means in practice:

- Make the image at least 1,080 pixels wide.
- Keep it no taller than 3:4. A 1,080 × 1,440 portrait is the tallest you can go without cropping.
- Square templates are safe. Very tall stacked memes are not, so split them into a carousel.

## X (Twitter) Image Size and File Limits

X's [help article on posting photos and GIFs](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/posting-gifs-and-pictures) is short and specific:

- Photos can be up to 5MB.
- Animated GIFs can be up to 5MB on mobile and up to 15MB on the web.
- Accepted formats are GIF, JPEG and PNG. BMP, TIFF and other formats are not accepted.
- When you post a single photo with an aspect ratio between 2:1 and 3:4, it displays in full in the post.

X doesn't publish a recommended pixel size. The page only says photos are scaled automatically for display. If you're posting one meme, anything from a wide 2:1 to a portrait 3:4 will show uncropped, which covers almost every common template. Tall vertical memes outside 3:4 risk being trimmed in the timeline preview.

## Reddit Meme Size

Reddit's help center doesn't publish a file size limit or recommended dimensions for image posts. We searched its posting articles and found nothing. The only image size rule we could find is for images in comments, which have a maximum height or width of 240 pixels according to [Reddit's comment image article](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/10516331142932-How-do-I-add-images-in-comments). That limit applies to comments only.

Many third-party sites quote a figure for Reddit image posts, but none of them link to an official Reddit page, so we're leaving it out. Individual subreddits can also set their own rules about image posts, so check the sidebar of the community you're posting to.

## WhatsApp Image Quality

WhatsApp doesn't publish pixel dimensions or a file size for photos. What it does document, in its [HD photos and videos FAQ](https://faq.whatsapp.com/759301289012856/), is the choice between Standard and HD quality.

- You can pick Standard or HD each time you send a photo in a chat.
- To make HD the default, go to Settings > Storage and data > Media upload quality and choose HD quality.
- HD isn't available for status updates or profile pictures.
- WhatsApp notes that your connection affects the quality of media sent and received, even in HD.

For memes, switch to HD before sending anything with small text. Standard quality is fine for big, bold captions, but fine print and thin outlines suffer first.

## Facebook Meme Size

Facebook's help center doesn't give recommended pixel dimensions for regular photo posts. It does give file guidance in its [photo upload troubleshooting article](https://www.facebook.com/help/167931376599294/):

- Recommended formats are JPEG, BMP, PNG, GIF and TIFF.
- If you upload a PNG, keep it below 1MB. Facebook says PNG files larger than 1MB may appear pixelated.
- Keep photos below 15MB.

That PNG note matters for memes, because meme editors usually export PNG. If your PNG is over 1MB, either reduce its dimensions or save a high-quality JPEG copy for Facebook.

## TikTok Photo Post Size

TikTok's [guide to making a post](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/making-a-post) says you can upload up to 35 photos in one photo post and crop them in the editor before posting. It doesn't list recommended dimensions, an aspect ratio, a file size limit or accepted formats for photo posts.

Since TikTok is built around a tall vertical screen, a vertical image will fill more of it than a square one. Use the crop step to check that your captions aren't cut off before you post.

## Discord File Size Limit

Discord's [File Attachments FAQ](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/25444343291031-File-Attachments-FAQ) says the maximum upload for users without Nitro is 20MB. Images larger than 20MB get shared as a download instead of showing inline. The same page notes that Discord is experimenting with upload limits and some users may be able to send larger files, so your own limit could differ.

A normal meme is nowhere near 20MB. If you hit the limit, the file is almost certainly a giant uncompressed PNG straight from a phone screenshot or a high-resolution photo. Resize it.

## PNG or JPEG for Memes

Choose the format based on what the image is mostly made of.

**PNG** keeps sharp edges perfectly. It's the better choice for memes with flat colors, screenshots, text on plain backgrounds and anime frames with large areas of solid color. The tradeoff is file size, which gets large on detailed photos.

**JPEG** is far smaller for photographs, but its compression smears hard edges. Text outlines are exactly the kind of edge it struggles with, so you may see faint blocky noise around captions.

**GIF** is only worth it for animation. For a still meme it's the wrong tool.

A sensible default: download as PNG, post the PNG where size limits allow, and save a JPEG copy only when a platform's limit or Facebook's 1MB PNG note pushes you to.

## Compression Tips That Keep Captions Sharp

Every platform here re-compresses images in some way, so give them a clean file to start with.

1. **Start at the right width.** For Instagram, export at 1,080 pixels wide or larger. Upscaling a tiny image afterward never brings detail back.
2. **Make text bigger than you think.** Platform compression hits small text and thin strokes first. A slightly heavier outline survives better. Our guide to [Impact font memes and readable captions](https://www.meme0.com/blog/2026-10-21-impact-font-meme-history-readable-captions) covers sizing and stroke in more detail.
3. **Keep captions away from the edges.** Cropping trims edges first. Leave some margin above the top text and below the bottom text.
4. **Don't re-save a JPEG repeatedly.** Each save adds more artifacts. Edit from the original and export once.
5. **Avoid screenshots of memes.** Re-screenshotting a meme adds a second layer of compression and often a status bar. Share the downloaded file instead.

## FAQ

### What is the best size for a meme on Instagram?

At least 1,080 pixels wide with an aspect ratio between 1.91:1 and 3:4. Instagram's help center says images in that range keep their resolution, and anything outside the ratio gets cropped.

### What is the maximum image size on X (Twitter)?

X's help center says photos can be up to 5MB, and animated GIFs up to 5MB on mobile or 15MB on the web. It accepts GIF, JPEG and PNG.

### Should I save memes as PNG or JPEG?

PNG keeps text and outlines sharper, so it's usually the better choice for memes. Use JPEG when the file is too large for a platform, such as Facebook, which recommends keeping PNG uploads below 1MB.
